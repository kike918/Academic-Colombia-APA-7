## High-value findings

### 1. Time-period comparability

One table presents:
- second-semester production for 2020;
- second-semester production for 2021;
- second-semester production for 2022;
- annual production for 2023.

The accompanying prose describes a progression across these values.

This should be flagged because annual 2023 production is not directly comparable with second-semester values from prior years without qualification or normalization.

Expected behavior:
- `academic-critical-review` identifies incompatible comparison bases;
- `academic-tables-figures` requires period labels to remain explicit;
- `academic-final-review` does not allow the visual/table presence to mask the methodological problem.

### 2. Theory application vs proof

The paper applies comparative advantage, Heckscher-Ohlin and new trade theory to the dispute. The framework should distinguish:
- explanatory framework;
- demonstrated causal mechanism;
- policy interpretation.

Theories should not be treated as empirical proof of why a government acted.

### 3. Institutional/legal precision

Statements about CAN dispute mechanisms should prefer exact institutional/legal terminology and authoritative CAN/TJCA sources. Generic descriptions should be flagged when the activity depends on legal precision.

### 4. Quantitative evidence

Large production/export values and quota figures need direct source-to-table traceability. A reference list alone is insufficient when a specific number drives the argument.
