## Findings

### 1. Current-fact verification

The document discusses events and institutional states in 2026. A functioning workflow should trigger freshness verification rather than rely on model memory.

Independent review confirmed selected claims, but that verification is external to the submitted artifact unless cited there.

### 2. Evidence density

Several pages make detailed assertions about WTO negotiations, Colombia's diplomatic position, OECD governance and policy effects with sparse or no inline citations.

Expected behavior:
- map important claims to specific sources;
- prefer official WTO/OECD/Colombian government sources for institutional facts;
- distinguish sourced fact from analysis/opinion;
- avoid treating a bibliography as automatic paragraph-level evidence.

### 3. Tone and overclaim control

Expressions equivalent to "existential", "hegemonic actors", "magna diplomatic crusade" or highly deterministic claims about national policy should be flagged when they are not required by the evidence or academic tone.

### 4. Artifact QA

Rendering demonstrates that `academic-document-auditor` must keep layout and evidence findings separate:
- layout can PASS;
- evidence integrity can still FAIL.
