## High-value findings

### 1. Source hierarchy works

The artifact includes relatively strong sources such as:
- international-trade textbooks;
- Harvard Business Review;
- academic/institutional books.

It also includes weaker sources such as:
- Scribd;
- Site123;
- Studocu;
- study-summary websites.

A functioning `academic-source-evaluator` should not treat those sources as equivalent.

### 2. Citation reconciliation is necessary

At least one web source cited in the body is not clearly reconciled with the final reference list, while several final references have incomplete publication metadata.

Expected behavior:
- `academic-citation-manager` reports citation/reference mismatches;
- it does not invent missing metadata;
- it recommends replacing weak sources with stronger primary/academic sources where the claim matters.

### 3. Collaborative consistency

Different contributor sections use noticeably different levels of sourcing and academic precision. The framework should normalize the final quality gate without erasing authorship attribution or silently rewriting all sections as one voice.

### 4. Requirement ambiguity

The stated objectives mention constructing infographic timelines. The inspected PDF is primarily a long-form textual analysis. Without the official guide/rubric, the framework must flag a potential requirement gap rather than assume the objective was fully satisfied.
